Mont-Saint-Michel from Paris by Train: Routes, Times and Tickets

No train reaches the island itself — but the rail trip is straightforward once you know which station to aim for. Here's how it works, and how to read it for your own date.

By EugeneUpdated October 20267-minute read
Get the audio guide Find trains on SNCF Connect

There is no direct train to Mont-Saint-Michel — no railway line reaches the island. You take a train to the nearest station, then finish the last stretch by bus and a free shuttle. From Paris-Montparnasse station to the village gate, allow roughly 4 to 5 hours depending on the route, the transfer and the wait for the shuttle. The connections aren't hard, but if it's your first time in Normandy and Brittany and you don't speak French, leave a buffer for them.

The train only solves half the trip — getting you to the island's gate. After that the Mont itself begins, and this is where the self-guided TouringBee island audio guide earns its place: an offline map and a story at each stop, one payment, a year's access, no subscription. There's a full section on it below.

In short: what the options look like

Not "three seasons, three routes" — think of it as two end stations and whatever SNCF shows for your date:

Paris → Rennes (TGV) → Pontorson (TER) → bus + shuttleWhen there's no direct train but you'd rather reach PontorsonTwo changes; go by what SNCF offers

In every case the train leaves from Paris-Montparnasse. The exact connections depend on the date — follow what SNCF Connect shows when you enter your travel day.

How it works

Mont-Saint-Michel has no station of its own. The nearest is Pontorson-Mont-Saint-Michel, about 9 km from the island. The second anchor is Rennes, a major station with frequent fast trains from Paris and a direct coach to the Mont. So in practice you aim for one of two end points: Pontorson (closer to the island) or Rennes (more frequent trains). Which of them works — a direct train, a train with a change, or a train plus coach — depends on the date, and that's exactly what SNCF Connect shows once you put your travel day in.

Reaching Pontorson — the nearest station

Pontorson is the closest station to the island. From Paris there are two ways to reach it, and the date decides which:

  • A direct TER from Paris-Montparnasse to Pontorson, running via Villedieu-les-Poêles with no change. This direct train doesn't run every day — it's tied to the season and the day of the week, so check availability for your date.
  • With a change at Rennes: first the TGV Paris → Rennes, then a regional TER Rennes → Pontorson. For example, on the SNCF timetable for 20 October 2026: depart Paris-Montparnasse 08:43 → Rennes 10:25 → Pontorson 12:14. That's a train to Pontorson, not the Rennes coach — sometimes it's the easier option.

From Pontorson station, a separate ground service covers the last stretch to the island (see "The last stretch" below).

Via Rennes — the most frequent option

If there's no convenient direct train to Pontorson on your date, the most reliable way is through Rennes:

  • TGV Paris-Montparnasse → Rennes: roughly 1h25–2h depending on the train.
  • Direct coach Rennes → Mont-Saint-Michel: it leaves from the bus station (gare routière) right beside Rennes railway station and takes about 1h15. The terminus is by the mainland car park and visitor centre — not at the gate itself. It runs year-round; departures are few and vary by season, with fewer in winter, so check the timetable and your return in advance.

The upside is the frequent Paris–Rennes trains and the chance to line up a return connection. The downside is the train-to-coach change.

The last stretch: three different services, don't mix them up

This is the easiest place to get confused. Depending on your route you use one or two of three separate services — each with its own stops and tickets:

  1. Coach Rennes → Mont-Saint-Michel. An intercity coach from Rennes bus station; the terminus is by the mainland car park and visitor centre. A separate ticket, around €15 one way.
  2. Bus from Pontorson station → the Mont's car park. It leaves from right outside Pontorson station and reaches the mainland car-park area in about 25 minutes. Paid, ticket from the operator; a limited number of runs a day.
  3. The free Le Passeur shuttle. It runs only on the mainland: from the car park to a stop about 350 m from the walls (not to the gate itself). Free and frequent — roughly every 5–12 minutes.

In other words, the Rennes and Pontorson buses bring you to the mainland, and you cover the final hop to the island on the free Le Passeur shuttle — or on foot (a flat 2.5 km across the bridge, 30–45 minutes, with the Mont in front of you the whole way).

Le Passeur shuttle hours (they matter for the evening)

The free shuttle runs on a seasonal timetable (per the official site, montsaintmichel.gouv.fr):

PeriodHours
November–February08:30–22:00
March–October (outside peak periods)07:30–23:00
July–August07:00–01:00

During school holidays the timetable may shift to a different seasonal band. The interval is roughly 5–12 minutes. If you're heading back in the evening, keep these hours in mind — miss the last shuttle and it's a 2.5 km walk in the dark.

Costs, and how to go cheaper

The total is the train plus the last leg. Ground transport: the Rennes coach is about €15 one way, the Pontorson bus is a separate low-cost ticket, and the Le Passeur shuttle is free. The big variable is the train: TGV fares depend heavily on how early you book. The cheapest seats are on the early-morning OUIGO services, where fares can start from around €10–19 if booked ahead. The rule is simple: the earlier you book and the more flexible your timing, the cheaper it gets. Trains and combined tickets are easiest to compare and buy on SNCF Connect, where you'll also see which option — direct to Pontorson, via Rennes by train, or via Rennes by coach — is available on your date.

Getting back to Paris

When you plan the day, find the last convenient return connection first, then build the rest around it. The last evening trains from Pontorson and from Rennes to Paris, and the last coach from the Mont, are what decide how much time you really have on the island — not the abbey's opening hours. Check the return direction for your date before you book the outbound.

Can you do it as a day trip? A sample day

Yes, but it's a long day. As an example, here's the Pontorson routing on 20 October 2026 (check it for your own date):

  • 08:43 — depart Paris-Montparnasse.
  • 10:25 — Rennes, change to the regional train.
  • 12:14 — arrive Pontorson.
  • ~12:40–13:00 — bus from the station to the mainland car park (~25 min) and the free Le Passeur shuttle to the stop 350 m from the gate.
  • ~13:00–16:30 — a few hours on the island: up the Grande Rue, the abbey, the ramparts.
  • Later afternoon — the last convenient return train (look it up in advance — it's what sets your limit).

All told, the round trip easily runs 8–10 hours for a few hours on the island itself. That isn't necessarily a bad choice, but it's a trade-off worth knowing before you go. If you want to catch both the tide and the island without the day crowds, consider a night on the island or by the car park — see our where-to-stay guide.

The village and the abbey

One point that matters to day-trippers in particular: entering the island village itself is free — you can walk up the Grande Rue and along the ramparts freely. If you want to go inside the abbey, that ticket is bought separately, so check its opening hours and availability for your date (details in our abbey tickets guide).

Practical tips

  • Book the train ahead — it's cheaper, and popular dates sell out.
  • Check the return connection first, then plan the day.
  • Leave a buffer between the train and the bus at the transfer.
  • Put your date into SNCF Connect and see what's actually offered: direct to Pontorson, via Rennes by train, or via Rennes by coach.
  • In the evening, keep the Le Passeur hours in mind or you risk the 2.5 km walk in the dark.
  • Download the audio guide and offline map in advance.
  • Wear proper shoes: the island is all stairs and worn stone.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a direct train from Paris to Mont-Saint-Michel?

No — no railway line reaches the island. The nearest station is Pontorson-Mont-Saint-Michel (about 9 km), then it's by road. A direct Paris–Pontorson train exists, but not every day.

How long does the trip from Paris take?

From Montparnasse station to the village gate, about 4–5 hours including the transfer and the last stretch.

Is it better to go to Pontorson or to Rennes?

Pontorson is closer to the island, but the direct train isn't always there. Rennes has more frequent trains and a direct coach to the Mont. Go by what SNCF shows for your date.

How do I get from the station to the island?

From Rennes, a direct coach to the mainland car park. From Pontorson, a bus to the car park (~25 min). The final mainland hop is the free Le Passeur shuttle to a stop 350 m from the walls, or a 2.5 km walk.

Can I do it as a day trip?

Yes, but it's 8–10 hours of travel for a few hours on the island. Check the last return connection first — it sets your limit.

Where do I buy tickets?

Trains and combined tickets on SNCF Connect, which also shows the available option for your date. Bus tickets are usually bought from the operator; the Le Passeur shuttle is free.

The takeaway

Mont-Saint-Michel is an easy trip by train from Paris, as long as you don't look for a non-existent direct service to the island and instead aim for the nearest station: Pontorson (when a convenient train runs) or Rennes (more frequent trains and a direct coach). From the mainland it's the free shuttle or a 2.5 km walk. Put your date into SNCF Connect, check the return connection and the shuttle hours, and the rest is just the trip itself.

And so the island doesn't come down to "up and back," download the audio guide before you set off and walk the Mont at your own pace, with all its stories.
