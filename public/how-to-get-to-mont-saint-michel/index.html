How to Get to Mont-Saint-Michel: Train, Bus, Car & Shuttle

Mont-Saint-Michel sits in a bay on the Normandy–Brittany border, far from big cities — but getting there is simpler than it looks. Here's every route, with 2026 prices and travel times.

By EugeneUpdated July 20266-minute read

Mont-Saint-Michel stands in a bay on the border of Normandy and Brittany, away from the big cities — and there are several ways to reach it. The good news: whichever you choose, the last stretch is the same — a free shuttle from the mainland car park to the island itself. This guide lays out how to get there from Paris and from the region, what it costs, and how long it takes.

We'll go through each option — the train, the bus via Rennes, driving, and the final "Le Passeur" shuttle. By the end you'll know which route suits you, and you won't lose time to queues and awkward connections. How to get there, at a glance

Direct train from Paris
The "Mont-Saint-Michel train" runs from Paris-Montparnasse to Pontorson–Le-Mont-Saint-Michel, then a bus to the island. One ticket covers train + bus. Runs daily April–October and at weekends year-round. Journey from about 3h53min.
Via Rennes (year-round)
TGV from Paris-Montparnasse to Rennes (about 2 hours), then a Keolis bus from Rennes station straight to Mont-Saint-Michel (about 1h15). Buses run every day, all year.
By car
You can't drive to the island. Park at the mainland car park, ~2.5 km from the rock, then take the free shuttle or walk the causeway (about 45 minutes).
Free shuttle (Le Passeur)
A free shuttle links the car park and the island. Runs 7:30 to midnight, about every 12 minutes, a few minutes each way. Drops you ~350 m from the entrance.
Nearest station
Pontorson–Le-Mont-Saint-Michel, 9 km away. A shuttle bus connects the station to Mont-Saint-Michel year-round.

By train from Paris

The simplest — and often cheapest — option is the direct "Mont-Saint-Michel train" from Paris-Montparnasse. You take the train, get off at Pontorson–Le-Mont-Saint-Michel, and change to a bus that drops you at the island entrance. One ticket covers both the train and the bus, so there's nothing extra to buy.

A price guide for 2026: a single from Paris was about €32 one way (train plus bus), children 4–11 €18.30, under-4s free. From the Normandy region, the ticket to the island is about €20. A nice bonus: this ticket gives a €1.50 discount on abbey entry. Check current fares and times on sncf-connect.com.

One scheduling catch: the direct train runs daily from April to October and at weekends year-round. If you're travelling on a winter weekday, plan your route via Rennes (below).

Via Rennes: all year round

If there's no direct train on your dates, the most reliable option is via Rennes. First a TGV high-speed train from Paris-Montparnasse to Rennes — about 2 hours. From Rennes station, a Keolis bus runs straight to Mont-Saint-Michel in about 1h15. Buses run every day, all year, and the final stop is right by the car park, the shuttles and the visitor centre. You can book the Paris–Rennes TGV on SNCF.

Check the bus timetable in advance and line it up with your train: there are only a few runs a day, and the gap between train and bus can be inconvenient. See the current schedule at keolis-armor.com, in the Mont-Saint-Michel section.

By car

Driving is handy if you're coming from the region or combining Mont-Saint-Michel with other stops in Normandy and Brittany. But you can't drive to the island — private traffic is blocked. In your sat-nav, set the destination to "Parking du Mont-Saint-Michel" — the large mainland car park about 2.5 km from the rock, with 4,000 spaces.

The car park is paid, and you pay before you leave. In 2026 the price depends on the season and how long you stay. For a standard car, rough guides:

From the Paris airports

If you're flying into Paris and heading straight for Mont-Saint-Michel, first get to Gare Montparnasse.

From Charles-de-Gaulle (CDG), the easiest way is the RER B into central Paris, then the metro to Montparnasse.

From Orly (ORY), the simplest route is metro line 14, which links the airport directly with Paris; then a short connection to Montparnasse.

From there, take either the direct Mont-Saint-Michel train or a TGV to Rennes with the bus connection.

The free Le Passeur shuttle

However you arrive — train, bus or car — the last stretch is the same. A free shuttle, "Le Passeur", runs between the mainland zone (car park and visitor centre) and the island. It operates from 7:30 to midnight, leaves about every 12 minutes, and takes a few minutes. Its cost is already built into the parking ticket, and anyone arriving by bus, train or bike rides free too.

The shuttle drops you about 350 m from the island entrance — the rest is on foot, as cars and buses don't reach the gates. If you'd rather walk, it's about 45 minutes along the causeway from the car park to the island: a lovely stroll with the rock growing in front of you.

Walk or shuttle?

Both are good — it just depends on your plans.

Take the shuttle if you:

  • are travelling with children;
  • are short on time;
  • arrived in rain or strong wind;
  • don't fancy the near-2.5 km walk.

Walk if you:

  • want the best photos of Mont-Saint-Michel;
  • enjoy a calm stroll;
  • arrived in good weather.

Many travellers walk out to the island and take the shuttle back. You get the panoramas without tiring yourself out at the end of the day. Either way, you can enjoy our self-guided audio guide, which tells you the stories on the way to the rock.

TouringBee tip: if the weather allows, walk at least one direction. The causeway gives the finest views of Mont-Saint-Michel — ones you'll never catch from the shuttle window. You can always ride the free Le Passeur back.

How much time to allow

From Paris on the direct train it's about 4 hours one way, including the final bus and shuttle. Via Rennes it works out about the same: roughly 2 hours on the TGV plus 1h15 on the bus plus the connections. If you're going for the day, plan an early start and check the return times in advance — the last convenient train or bus can leave earlier than you'd think.

Practical tips

  • Check whether the direct train runs on your dates; in winter weekdays, plan via Rennes.
  • Line up the Rennes bus with your train in advance — there aren't many runs.
  • In your sat-nav set "Parking du Mont-Saint-Michel", not the island itself.
  • Parking is paid on the way out; summer rates are higher.
  • The Le Passeur shuttle is free and runs until midnight — you can happily stay for sunset.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to get to Mont-Saint-Michel from Paris?

Usually the direct "Mont-Saint-Michel train" from Montparnasse: one ticket covers the train and the final bus. From about €32 one way — check the current fare on sncf-connect.com.

Is there a direct train every day?

The direct train runs daily from April to October and at weekends year-round. The rest of the time it's easier via Rennes with a bus connection.

How long does it take from Paris?

About 4 hours one way — whether by direct train or via Rennes, including the final bus and shuttle.

Can you drive up to the island?

No. Cars are left at the mainland car park 2.5 km away; from there it's the free shuttle or a walk.

Is the shuttle to the island paid?

No, the Le Passeur shuttle is free. For those who came by car, its cost is already built into the parking ticket.

How far is the walk?

From the car park to the island along the causeway is about 2.5 km, roughly 45 minutes. The shuttle drops you closer — about 350 m from the entrance.

Can you arrive in the evening?

Yes. On summer evenings the crowds thin out and Mont-Saint-Michel looks its best in the low sun. Le Passeur runs almost to midnight, so you can happily stay after the main flow of visitors.

Can you travel with a suitcase?

Yes. If you come by train or bus you can take a case on the shuttle. Inside the island the streets are narrow and cobbled, so rolling a big suitcase over the stones isn't much fun.

The takeaway

Getting to Mont-Saint-Michel only looks complicated on paper. In practice the choice is simple: the direct train from Paris if it runs on your dates; the Rennes route the rest of the time; or a car if you're combining it with other stops in the region. And the final stretch is always the same — the free shuttle or a short walk along the causeway to the rock.
